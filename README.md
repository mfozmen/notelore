> A note-taking assistant you talk to. Your notes stay plain Markdown on your disk.

Tell it things; it writes tidy notes. Ask later, *"Which database did we pick for Mopsos?"*, and it answers from your notes.

- **Plain files:** your notes are Markdown, readable in any editor or Obsidian; export them from the Notes screen
- **Your choice of model:** your ChatGPT plan, Claude, GPT, Gemini, or a local model with Ollama
- **Talk or type:** voice mode listens and reads answers aloud
- **Every device in sync** through your Google Drive: Windows, macOS and Android
- **English and Turkish**

> [!NOTE]
> Notelore is early and under active development. See the [roadmap](https://github.com/mfozmen/notelore/milestones?direction=asc&sort=due_date).

## Install

Download the app from the [releases page](https://github.com/mfozmen/notelore/releases):

| Platform | File | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Windows | `notelore-app-<version>-windows-x64-setup.exe` | Installs for you only (your own folder, no admin rights), so each person on a shared computer has their own Notelore in their own Windows account; an update moves an earlier all-users install out of Program Files after one admin prompt (say no and the old copy stays, removable from Windows' Apps). Windows may warn about an unknown publisher (not code-signed yet). |
| macOS | `notelore-app-<version>-macos.dmg` | Needs macOS 14 (Sonoma) or later. Drag `notelore.app` onto *Applications*. The first time, right-click it and choose *Open* (not notarized yet). |
| Android | `notelore-app-<version>-android.apk` | Allow installing from your browser or file manager. |

The app checks for a new release every time it opens (and every few hours while it stays open), says what the new version brings in a line (with *What's new* for the rest) and offers *Update* (on Android you confirm in the system installer). A new version is offered only once its file for your device and that file's checksum are up, never while the release is still being built.

## Get started

1. Open Notelore and *Sign in with Google* with your Google (Gmail) account: this also connects Google Drive, where your notes are backed up and synced.
2. Pick a model, step by step: *Choose* the provider (each option is a card saying who it suits; the chosen one shows what it costs, where your notes go, how good it is and the effort to set it up; one you connected before is used again at once), *Connect* it (its cost and, for a key, numbered steps to get one), and *Done* says what is connected and shows the model's own one-line hello, proof that it works (that one message goes to the provider, a few tokens; if it fails or takes longer than 20 seconds, you are back on *Connect* with the reason). A bar on top shows the step; back goes one step back and keeps your choice. The ChatGPT plan stays one tap, straight from *Choose*:
   - **ChatGPT plan** (desktop and phone), the default: *Continue with ChatGPT* and sign in, one tap; on a phone the browser hands you back to Notelore by itself. No extra charges, within your plan's limits. It needs a **Plus or Pro** plan: only those can be shared with other apps. A free ChatGPT account is told so plainly, and offered the free way in right there: *Get a free Gemini key* opens Google AI Studio with your Google account; copy the key, come back, and Notelore puts it in the box for you (you still tap *Connect*). On the free tier Google may use your requests to improve its products.
   - Beside it, for your own API key or a local model:
     - **Claude, GPT or Gemini:** paste an API key; numbered steps show where to get one, and a button opens the provider's key page in the browser. Copy the key there and come back: Notelore puts it in the box for you (only a key shaped like that provider's, and the clipboard is read only on that return). Keys stay in your device's keystore.
     - **On this device** (Android, Windows, macOS): no key and no account. The model (Qwen3 4B, about 2.5 GB) downloads once, checked against its SHA-256, and continues where it stopped if the connection drops; it then runs inside Notelore through llama.cpp, offline: no AI provider sees your notes (links you send are still read from the web, and Drive still backs up the notes if you connected it). It gets a shorter set of instructions than a cloud model, so a phone starts answering sooner. Answers are simpler than a cloud model's and slow on a phone. It is offered on Windows, macOS and on Android phones with a 64-bit system and at least 6 GB of memory; if a device still cannot run it, the chat says so.
     - **Ollama** (desktop): no key needed. Steps show how to install it and pull a model (a button opens ollama.com), and connecting checks that Ollama answers and starts on a model it has (a Notelore fine-tune first, the newest, else Qwen3); if it has none, it says which command pulls one (`ollama pull qwen3:4b`), in text you can copy.

   A key you set up before keeps working.
3. Talk to it: *"Mopsos için not al: veritabanı olarak SQLite seçtik."*
4. Ask it later: *"Mopsos'ta hangi veritabanını seçmiştik?"*

Notelore starts on the cheapest model your provider offers (with Gemini, on gemini-2.5-flash, which a free key has quota for); switch any time with the model chip above the message box.

> [!TIP]
> Gemini is free with a key from a Google AI Studio project that has no billing, and Ollama is free too. Claude and GPT through their APIs are billed per use; a Claude Pro or Max subscription cannot be used in other apps.

## Using it

- **Its mark:** a note you talk to, a white card with a speech tail on lagoon teal; on Android it follows the system's themed icons.
- **On the desktop:** a side rail (with labels when the window is wide enough) replaces the phone's bottom bar, and the chat is a readable column.
- **Chat:** An empty chat shows a few examples; tap one to put it in the message box. Notelore takes notes rather than making conversation: after you tell it something, it says in a sentence what it saved and in which note, and leaves it there (it still asks when it cannot tell where something belongs, and answers questions about your notes in full). When you say a choice was made ("we picked SQLite for Mopsos"), it is recorded as that note's decision, so "what did we decide about the database?" finds it later. While the assistant works, a playful line keeps you company ("went to make coffee"); *Stop* ends a turn. Offline, or over your provider's usage limit, a message waits ("Waiting to send") instead of failing: it is kept across restarts and goes out by itself when Notelore can reach the model again (on return to the app, every minute, or with *Try now*), in the order you wrote them. A question that names a project or topic ("what do my Notelore notes say about sync?") is answered from that note only. You can keep writing while it works: what you add joins its answer. A message you have not sent yet stays in the box when you visit another tab. When the assistant asks something with a few likely answers, they show as buttons under its question: one tap answers, and typing still works. The paperclip beside the message box (files up to 20 MB) attaches text files (txt, md, csv, logs and the like) and PDFs (their text, read on the device): type what they are about first if you like, and the assistant files their content into the right notes, asking when it is unclear where it belongs. Images (PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP, up to 5 MB in one message) go to the model as pictures with ChatGPT, Claude, GPT and Gemini; Ollama says it cannot look at them. An image is sent only when nothing else is on its way, and never waits offline: if the network is gone, the message says to send it again, and no picture is kept on the device. A scanned PDF with no text in it is refused with a note, and nothing is sent. An audio file (a voice memo) is heard on the device by Whisper and goes as its words: WAV everywhere, and on Android also M4A/AAC, MP3, Ogg and FLAC through the phone's own decoder. Before Whisper is downloaded, in a format the device cannot open, or with no words in it, it is refused with a note and nothing is sent; Notelore never guesses what a recording says. The original of a recording or a PDF is kept beside your notes (in `attachments/<date>/`, never over another file), linked from the note it was filed into, and backed up through Google Drive with the notes; a copy changed on two devices keeps both, the other in `.notelore/history/`. Answers show formatted: bold, lists and links as they read in the notes, links open in the browser; a selection can run across a whole answer. A copy button under each message and answer puts its text on the clipboard: an answer as plain words (no Markdown marks, a link's address after its words), so it reads right in a messenger or a mail. A button under your last message sends it again with one tap. When an answer fails, a short note says why in plain words (offline, a refused key, a busy or troubled service) with *Try again* right beside it; if that answer had already saved notes, the note says so, so nothing is saved twice. With ChatGPT, GPT or Claude it can look things up on the web when that helps get a note right (the author of a book you mention, say); turn it off in Settings. Anthropic bills each search.
- **Links:** send a link (paste it, or on Android use *Share → Notelore* from the browser or any app, then review it in the message box and tap *Send*) and Notelore reads the page and files the link into the note it fits, with what you wrote about it taking precedence. It asks only when the page cannot be read or no note clearly fits, and never guesses what an unreadable page says. Only the page's text is read (a few links per message, with a size and time limit). The desktop has no share target: paste the link into the chat.
- **Voice:** one button next to the message box, like a messenger's: with text it sends, empty it records. Hold it and talk; let go and what you said is sent, and the answer is read aloud. Or tap it once: it keeps listening and turns into a Stop button, and a tap on Stop sends what you said (by keyboard, hold Space on the focused button; a screen reader taps once to start and again to send). Typing still works. On first start Notelore downloads Whisper by itself in the background (about 375 MB, once per device; not bundled, so updates stay small; an interrupted download continues where it stopped). Whisper hears you on the device, offline, in any language (a press with only noise or silence sends nothing, never Whisper's notes such as "[MUSIC PLAYING]"); Settings shows the download and lets you pick the microphone (and list them again after plugging one in). Until it is there, voice mode uses the device's speech recognition (on Windows that needs the speech pack for your language, and Windows has no Turkish one). English product and technical terms in Turkish speech are written in their usual spelling ("deyta beys" becomes "database").
- **Related notes:** Things mentioned in passing, such as a book you are reading or a film to watch, go as entries into a collection note ("Books I'm reading"), not a note each, unless you ask for one. Before starting a new note, the assistant checks your projects and topics (both kinds) for one it belongs with, by shared title words and matching lines, and adds to that note instead. When two notes belong together, it suggests merging them and does so only after you agree: everything of the one note that the other lacks is added (duplicates once), and the merged note moves to `_archive/`, from where the *Archive* button restores it.
- **Reminders:** say "remind me on Friday at 10 to call the dentist" (or just tell a plan, "I'll watch the derby next week": it becomes a reminder too, at the event's time when the model can look it up, otherwise for the whole day) and the assistant adds it to the note it belongs to, under its own *Reminders* section (`- 2026-10-24 10:00: Call the dentist`; the time is optional). At its time Notelore notifies you on the device (Android, Windows, macOS), also after the device restarts and for reminders another device added through Drive; untimed ones come at 9:00. While the app lock is on, a notification only says that you have a reminder, so a locked phone shows nothing of your notes. Notelore asks for notifications the first time you ask for a reminder; if you said no, *Settings → Reminders → Allow notifications* asks again. On Android a reminder is exact where the system allows exact alarms, otherwise it may come a few minutes late. Or turn on *Settings → Reminders → Add to Google Calendar* (it asks for Google Calendar access once, separate from Drive): your reminders then go into a calendar of Notelore's own, "Notelore", in your Google Calendar, and Google reminds you on every device while Notelore sends no notification of its own, so nothing comes twice. Notelore only ever sees that one calendar, never your others; while the app lock is on, its events too only say that you have a reminder. A device where the calendar was turned on elsewhere offers *Sign in to Google Calendar*, so its own new reminders go there too. Turned off, its coming events are removed (to Google Calendar's trash) and your devices remind you again. The setting travels to your other devices through Drive.
- **Notes:** browse your projects and topics; on a wide window the open note sits beside the list. An open note reads as a page: its title, kind and tags, the current decisions as cards (earlier ones folded away), then what is coming, reminders (coming ones first, past ones marked) and todos as checkboxes with the late ones in red, and after them the notes as a dated timeline, newest first; the file itself stays plain Markdown. Links in notes open in your browser. An image stored on the device shows in the note; an image from the web shows as a box naming the site it comes from and loads only when you tap it, so a link planted in a note cannot quietly send your text anywhere. Deleting a note moves it to `_archive/` in the notes folder, with an Undo; the *Archive* button lists what was deleted and restores it (entries the assistant archived go back into their note). From the archive you can also delete a note for good, or empty the whole archive, each after a confirmation; with Google Drive on, the copies there are removed after 30 days.
- **Calendar:** a tab of its own shows what your notes plan: every reminder and every open todo with a date, read from the notes (nothing from other calendars). With no day picked it lists what is coming, late todos first; a month grid marks the days with something on them, and a tap on a day shows that day. A tap on an item opens its note. On a wide window the month and the list sit side by side.
- **Export, share and import:** an open note's share button exports it as Markdown (the note file itself, which Notelore can import again), PDF (set in Noto Sans, so every Turkish letter shows) or a web page (HTML). *Export notes* above the list does the same for several notes at once: one PDF or page for all, or one Markdown file each. On a phone the files go to the share sheet (WhatsApp, mail, Drive...); on the desktop to a file or folder you pick, never over a file already there. *Import notes* brings Markdown notes in, for example ones someone shared with you: a note whose name is taken comes in beside it as "<title> (2)", the same note again changes nothing, and anything that is not a Notelore note is refused. Import never overwrites a note. A picture from the web written into a note is never loaded in the exported page (its description shows instead), and in the app only when you tap it.
- **Settings:** language (System, English or Türkçe), a theme from six nature palettes (lagoon, pine forest, open sea, sunset, lavender field, northern lights) in light or dark, model and usage, Google Drive, an optional app lock, and an About card with the version, updates and log out. On a wide window the sections (model, voice, sync, appearance, app lock, about) are listed on the left, one shown at a time. *Settings → Model* lists every model you connected on this device, the one in use marked: tap another to switch to it (its key is checked again), remove one you no longer want (asked first; your notes and the conversation stay), or *Connect another model* to run the setup wizard again. Removing the one in use leaves none: the app stays open, your notes and the conversation with it, and the chat shows *Connect a model* where the message box was.
- **Usage:** for advanced users: turn on *Settings → Show advanced settings*, then *Usage* opens a page of its own. Pick a range (7, 14 or 30 days, or all) at the top. It shows what the model read and wrote (in tokens, pieces of words) in this conversation and in that range, as the provider reported them; each day as a bar split by model, in colours told apart with colour blindness (tap or drag along the chart to see each model's part of a day in words; with all time the chart shows the last 90 days kept); each model's share as a percentage with its own tokens and cost; what your messages came to (how many, model calls and tokens per message, the average answer time, tool calls, messages that wrote notes and the share that ended in an error; only counts are kept, never what you said); and an estimated cost from a small table of list prices (with the date they were taken); on the ChatGPT plan or a local model, where nothing is charged per token, it says so instead of showing $0.00. It is an estimate: your provider's bill is what counts. Where a provider or model gives nothing to go on, it says "not available" instead of guessing. The ChatGPT plan and a local Ollama model have no per-token charge. Everyday use never shows token counts.
- **App lock:** *Settings → App lock → Turn on* asks how Notelore should lock. The recommended choice is **your device's lock**: whatever unlocks the device (fingerprint, face, pattern or PIN on Android; Windows Hello; Touch ID or your account password on macOS) opens Notelore too, and Notelore stores no secret for it. A device without a screen lock says so, and you can pick a **pattern** (drawn through at least 4 of 9 dots, or tapped dot by dot) or a **PIN** (at least 4 digits) for Notelore instead, entered twice; the first ones anyone would try (one digit repeated, a run like 1234, an L, Z, U, C, 7 or the ring) are refused. Notelore asks for it every time it opens, before any note or chat shows, and again when it comes back after a minute or more in the background (a quick switch to another app does not lock); a note or dialog left open closes when it locks, so nothing of it shows over the lock. After five wrong patterns or PINs it makes you wait before the next try: 30 seconds, then a minute, then five minutes; the count survives a restart, a restart during a wait starts it again (so setting the clock ahead does not end it), and the right entry resets it (the device's lock counts its own). Only a salted hash of a pattern or PIN is kept, in the device's keystore; it is never stored as plain text and never synced. Turning the lock off asks for it first. A PIN or password set before this version keeps working until you turn it off and choose again. The lock never touches your notes: if you forget it, they are still Markdown files in the app's storage and on Drive.
- **Google Drive:** setup starts with *Sign in with Google*: Notelore needs a Google (Gmail) account, any account Google lets you sign in with, Workspace included, and without one it goes no further. Signing in connects Drive, which backs your notes up and keeps every device in sync, settings too (language, theme, provider and model; never your keys). The notes are kept in Drive's hidden app storage: it does not show in Drive, so nothing there can be edited, moved or deleted by accident, and Notelore sees only its own files. Notes from a version that kept them in a visible `Notelore` folder move there on the first sync (their old copies go to Drive's trash, and the emptied folder stays for you to delete); that update asks you to sign in to Google once more, to allow the hidden storage: a banner says so, and Settings → Google Drive says in plain words why sync waits. In Drive's settings, *Manage apps → Notelore → Delete hidden app data* removes the copy on Drive; your devices keep their notes and offer to put them back. After that the device opens without asking again, even offline; if access expires, the notes stay and *Settings → Google Drive → Connect* signs in again. If many notes disappear from Drive at once (or all of them, even one), a sync never deletes them here: a banner says how many and offers *Restore to Drive* or *Delete here too*, which names them and asks first; a single note removed in Drive moves to the device's history as before. Disconnecting signs out, and the next start asks for Google again. A build without Google sign-in (a development run without the client ids) skips this step and has no sync. Drive is the only backup: Android's own backup and phone-to-phone transfer take nothing of Notelore's, so keys never land on another device.

Your notes live in the app's own storage on every device (one copy, nothing for you to manage), and on Google Drive when you connect it. Updating from a version that kept them in a visible `~/Notelore/` folder moves them there once. Nothing is ever deleted for good: archived notes and both sides of a sync conflict are kept, and when Drive's copy of a note lacks lines this device has (a damaged copy, say), the device first keeps its own under `.notelore/kept/` in the notes folder, on that device only, for 30 days.
